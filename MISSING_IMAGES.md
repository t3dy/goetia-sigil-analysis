## 1. Sefer ha-Razim / Sefer Raziel HaMalakh — Amulet Diagram Page

**Displayed in:** Entry 3 — "Sefer ha-Razim & the Sword of Moses"

**What's needed:** A page from the 1701 Amsterdam printed edition of *Sefer Raziel HaMalakh* showing one of the illustrated amulet diagrams — the layered Hebrew divine-name grids, Kabbalistic circle diagrams, or the protective amulet for pregnant women. An image from a manuscript copy (pre-1701) would be preferable if available.

**Target sources:**
- **Center for Jewish Art (CJA), Hebrew University of Jerusalem** — has digitized copies of the 1701 Amsterdam edition: https://cja.huji.ac.il/browser.php?mode=set&id=38140
- **National Library of Israel (NLI)** — digitized early editions accessible via the NLI digital collection
- **Internet Archive** — search "Sefer Raziel" for scanned copies; the amulet pages are typically in the first section of the book
- A scan from the 1701 edition is public domain; contact NLI or CJA for a high-resolution export

**Notes:** The amulet page showing the circular protective inscription (shimshom or birth-protection amulet) is the most visually relevant and has been reproduced in scholarship (Trachtenberg, *Jewish Magic and Superstition*, 1939). Trachtenberg's book scans on archive.org may contain a reproduction.

## 2. Ars Notoria — Nota Diagram

**Displayed in:** Entry 4 — "Ars Notoria (Notory Art of Solomon)"

**What's needed:** One of the *notae* — the complex ritual figures central to the Ars Notoria practice. These are geometric-inscriptive diagrams combining circles, lines, symbols, and divine names in elaborate patterns. The figure for Grammar or Rhetoric is a good example.

**Target sources:**
- **Bodleian Library, Oxford — MS Bodley 951** — one of the most complete illustrated Latin manuscripts of the Ars Notoria; check Digital Bodleian (https://digital.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/) for availability
- **Bibliotheca Medicea Laurenziana, Florence — Plut.89 sup.38** — contains illustrated notae; check BML digital collections
- **British Library — Sloane MS 1712** — another illustrated Ars Notoria manuscript
- The published Matthias Castle edition (Inner Traditions, 2023) includes photographs of manuscript notae — the publisher or author may have licensed images for research use
- **BREPOLIS / HMML** — searched for digitized copies of the primary manuscripts

**Notes:** The 1657 Turner English translation (available on archive.org) does NOT include the nota drawings — they were omitted. You need the Latin manuscript, not the Turner translation. The notae are the most visually striking elements of the whole tradition and worth prioritizing.

## 3. Picatrix — Talisman Diagram Page

**Displayed in:** Entry 5 — "Picatrix (Ghāyat al-Ḥakīm)"

**What's needed:** A page from a Latin or Castilian manuscript of the Picatrix showing a planetary talisman figure — ideally the schematic figure for one of the seven planets with its accompanying inscription instructions.

**Target sources:**
- **Wellcome Collection — MS 517** — a Latin Picatrix manuscript; check Wellcome Digital Collections (https://wellcomecollection.org/) — Wellcome images are frequently CC BY 4.0
- **British Library — Sloane MS 3679** — Latin Picatrix; check BL Digitised Manuscripts (https://www.bl.uk/manuscripts/)
- **Bayerische Staatsbibliothek (BSB), Munich — Clm 276** — Latin Picatrix; check BSB Digital Collections (https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/)
- PICRYL had a result for a Picatrix manuscript page (https://picryl.com/media/manuscript-of-the-picatrix-da22f9) but the page returned 403 — try accessing directly via browser or contacting PICRYL

**Notes:** The Picatrix has no single canonical image tradition; what you want is a page that shows a diagrammatic talisman figure, not just dense text columns. Books III and IV of the Picatrix are most likely to have such figures.

## 4. Pseudomonarchia Daemonum (Johann Weyer, 1577)

**Displayed in:** Entry 9 — "Pseudomonarchia Daemonum (Johann Weyer)"

**What's needed:** A page from the appendix of Johann Weyer's *De Praestigiis Daemonum* (Basel: Oporinus, 1577 4th ed., or the 1563 first edition) showing the *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* text — ideally a page listing several demons with their descriptions, to contrast with the Goetia's sigil-accompanied entries.

**Target sources:**
- **Internet Archive** — search "De Praestigiis Daemonum Weyer" — multiple editions are scanned: https://archive.org/search?query=de+praestigiis+daemonum
- The 1563 Basel first edition and 1577 fourth edition are both in European library digitization projects
- **Bayerische Staatsbibliothek** and **Gallica (BnF)** both have early Weyer editions digitized
- A direct page image from a 16th-century printed book should be straightforwardly public domain

**Notes:** A page showing the demon entries in the Pseudomonarchia — with name, rank, abilities listed in running text but *no sigil* — would make the contrast with the Goetia's sigil-per-entry format visually obvious and analytically useful.
